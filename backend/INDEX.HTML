What are Vaccines?

Vaccines are a special medicine given to people to help their bodies fight off certain diseases. They work by teaching your body's immune system to recognize and attack germs, so you don't get sick from them later.

**Why Vaccination is Important:**

  • **Prevents diseases:** They stop you from getting serious and sometimes deadly diseases.
  • **Protects others:** When you get vaccinated, you also protect your family, friends, and community, especially those who can't get vaccinated themselves. This is called **herd immunity**.

**List of Common Vaccines:** Polio, Measles, Tetanus, Hepatitis, COVID-19, and many more are available.

**Vaccination Schedule:** It's important to get vaccines at the right time. There are schedules for infants, children, pregnant women, and the elderly. Your doctor or local health center can provide the correct schedule.

**Free vs Paid Vaccines:** The government provides many important vaccines for free. You can get these at government health centers. Some vaccines are available at private clinics for a fee.

**Safety & Myths:** Vaccines are safe. It's a myth that vaccines cause illnesses. Any minor side effects like a mild fever or pain at the injection site are normal signs that your body is building protection.

Emergency / Help

**What to Do After Vaccination:**

  • Keep an eye on the injection site for minor swelling or redness.
  • A mild fever or body aches are normal and can be treated with a cool compress.

**When to See a Doctor:**

Seek immediate medical help if you experience a severe allergic reaction, difficulty breathing, or high fever that doesn't go away.
